## Background
- Mismatch Negativity (MMN) is an Event-Related Potential (ERP) component present when sensory stimuli violate expectations
- A public dataset of three Common Marmosets was analyzed
- Electrocorticograms recorded neural activity during a passive listening task (auditory roving oddball paradigm)
- ERPs of Standards and Deviants are compared to explore MMN in Marmosets

## Mismatch Negativity
- Negative Peaks for Standards and Deviants at around 60ms after Stimulus Onset
- Standard Negative Peaks have poorer detection than Deviants
- Significant Electrodes are mostly found in the temporal lobe

## Discussion
- Findings confirm the presence of Mismatch Negativity (MMN) in common marmosets
- Compared to Humans, the latency of MMN is shorter (Humans ~100 ms), but longer than in cats (~40 ms)
- MMN may relate to brain network complexity
- Spatially, significant MMN activity was primarily observed in the temporal lobe, near the auditory cortex
- Coherence peaked around the stimulus onset asynchrony (SOA) Frequency, suggesting a rhythmic encoding of auditory input
- Evidence of phase resetting near SOA frequency was also found, although results are somewhat spurious
- Coherence is dispersed more widely across the cortex compared to MMN, potentially indicating processing of additional or distinct features beyond simple expectation violations
