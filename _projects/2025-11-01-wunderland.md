## Background
- Spoken language conveys information on multiple timescales (e.g., syllables, words, sentences)
- Neural tracking of spoken language can be quantified using linear (e.g. cerebro-acoustic coherence) or non-linear measures (e.g. Mutual Information)
- Here, we plan to explore the effects of primary vs. secondary language tracking comparing English and German language perception


## Processing

## Data Collection
- Data will be collected using a 64 Active Electrode EEG setup 
- Participants will listen to ~12 Minutes of the first chapter of Alice in Wonderland read in German and English (Counterbalanced across participants)
- Afterwards, 8 comprehension questions in German and English will be asked.
- We will also collect motor and language localizer data, and a set of behavioral measures to evaluate language competence.


## Discussion
- We expect to find Coherence and Mutual Information of EEG and Audio to be highest in the 2 to 12 Hz range, encoding for syllables, words, sentences in both English and German
- According to Cortical Efficiency Theory, less experienced listeners require higher cortical engagement (e.g. Reiterer et al., 2005): Effect of - - Nativeness in Neural Entrainment
- We may further uncover Effects of Language
