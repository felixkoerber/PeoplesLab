# Abstract

According to the predictive coding framework, mammalian brains make sense of the world by constantly comparing internal predictions with sensory input. This process is thought to be organized hierarchically, with information becoming increasingly abstract from primary sensory regions to higher cortical regions. The brief increase in negativity of scalp potentials following an unexpected stimulus after repeated identical stimuli is referred to as mismatch negativity. 

Changes in the typical mismatch negativity response have been linked to mental disorders, including schizophrenia, therefore, understanding how network dynamics influence expectations and mismatch negativity could elucidate the underlying causes of disorders. However, studying human network activity is constrained by both ethical and structural factors (e.g., the folded neocortex), thereby raising interest in finding potential model organisms. One promising candidate is the common marmoset (Callithrix jacchus), in which mismatch negativity has recently been described. Their relatively smooth, lissencephalic brain facilitates neural recordings of sources such as the auditory cortex. 

Here, publicly available ECoG data from three marmosets performing roving auditory oddball tasks are analyzed. Information network dynamics between selected electrodes are estimated using spectral multivariate transfer entropy for time windows before and after presentation of standards and deviants. Results demonstrate consistent pre-stimulus information transfer from the prefrontal to the superior temporal regions, primarily carried by alpha- and beta-band activity, indicative of top-down predictions of upcoming stimuli, with differences for standards and deviants. Differences in network dynamics between standards and deviants may reflect implicit learning of statistical regularities. Despite the lack of finding of post-stimulus networks, attributed to methodological constraints, this work provides preliminary evidence for preparatory hierarchical predictions of auditory stimuli in the common marmoset.
